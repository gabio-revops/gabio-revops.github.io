Forms Cookie Tracking B2C

Should I enable "Always create contact for new email addresses" on forms?

"Can you re-explain the reason for the 'create new contact from unknown email addresses' setting again? I need to explain it to our HubSpot coach. Here is his initial thought - What is the reason for them wanting to turn on that setting? I typically do not recommend turning that on unless we are using that form as an internal intake form, or for in-person events. The reason for this, is not only does it turn off pre-populating fields, it also impacts cookie tracking and being able to see webpage visits for contacts."

I totally hear the concern, and the coach is right that it used to be a real tradeoff. But I want to walk through both the "why" for this specific use case and also why I think the concern may be based on legacy behavior.

First, I agree there's a real problem being referenced here: historically in HubSpot, you had two options - leave cookie tracking on (which could overwrite contact records when someone submitted from a shared browser) or turn off cookie tracking entirely to force new contact creation (which then broke webpage visit tracking). That was a genuine no-win situation, and I believe the instinct comes from that era.

However, a while back, HubSpot released an update specifically to solve this - the "always create contacts for new email addresses" setting was built so you get both: new contacts created for every new email, AND page view tracking is still preserved. So the binary tradeoff doesn't fully apply to this setting the way it once did. Here's a community post where someone explained it.

That said, there is still behavior worth acknowledging - if a brand new person submits a form from a browser that already has someone else's cookie in it, that new contact won't inherit the prior browsing history from that cookie. So it's not a zero tradeoff. But given the audience here, I think it's still the right call.

Here's why I believe this setting is specifically right for a B2C remodeling business:

The cookie risk is different in B2B vs. B2C. In B2B, the more common problem is one person submitting with two different emails. In B2B you'd actually want HubSpot to recognize those as the same person, because you're selling to a company, not an individual. Consolidating those under one record is fine there.

In B2C, the bigger risk is two different people submitting from the same device. Think about a married couple sharing a home computer. One spouse was on the site a few months ago checking out the bathroom remodel pricing tool using their email. Now the other spouse sits down and fills out a form about a kitchen remodel using their email. With the cookie-based deduplication controlling things, HubSpot overwrites the first spouse's record with the second submission. You've now lost a lead entirely and potentially confused your follow-up because you've got the wrong person's info on the record.

For a remodeling company that sells to individuals and households, those are two separate potential entry points, and possibly two separate projects. Missing that second record is the worse outcome. That's why I recommended the setting - you capture every email as its own record and dedupe intentionally later if needed, rather than having HubSpot silently overwrite contacts based on shared browser history.
